How a verdict is read

gap ratio = free space / median vehicle width
SAFE Free gap at least 1.8 vehicle-widths at the nearest occupied depth band.
TIGHT A gap between 0.4 and 1.8; committing is a squeeze.
BLOCKED Under 0.4, or the centre line is covered, or a blocker closes inside 1.5 s.
UNKNOWN Not enough evidence: the stabiliser abstains until its 2 s window fills.

Nearest band wins. Road users are bucketed at 3 m and 8 m from an assumed height, and only the nearest occupied band is scored.

Normalised, so it travels. Dividing by the median vehicle width in that band makes the gap scale-invariant, which is why no camera calibration is needed.

Time-to-collision on top. A blocker closing inside 1.5 s downgrades the verdict regardless of the static gap, and a 2.0 s median with 0.5 s hysteresis stops a one-frame glitch from flipping the label.

How the decision is reached

same code path as the Python package
  1. 1

    Detect and track

    Six road-user classes, boxes smoothed with an EMA of 0.45, coasted through 0.4 s of dropout and drawn dashed while inferred.

  2. 2

    Project the corridor

    A trapezoid fixed in relative coordinates, so no camera calibration is required and the horizon is simply assumed at 0.45.

  3. 3

    Measure the gap

    The widest free lateral gap in the nearest band, divided by the median vehicle width in that band.

  4. 4

    Decide

    Fitted thresholds with centre-line and collision overrides, then a 2 s median and 0.5 s hysteresis.

Reading the numbers

Ranges are monocular estimates from assumed object heights and a 78° field of view. A gap ratio is a relative figure: it compares free space to the width of the vehicle beside it, not to a tape measure.
